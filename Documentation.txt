## Updated Architecture Flow

### 1. Ingestion & Integration

* **Source 1: Netezza** – Data is loaded into Databricks (likely as a staging Parquet/Delta layer or directly into memory).
* **Source 2 & 3: FCRP & IWM Oracle** – These are accessed via **Federated Catalogs**. This is efficient because you aren't moving the data until the Spark job actually requests it for the transformation.

### 2. Transformation (The 4 SQL Scripts)

* The scripts run in **Databricks Spark SQL**.
* They join the Netezza "loaded" data with the "live" Federated data from FCRP and Oracle.

### 3. Dual-Output Logic (Current STG Phase)

Your Spark job should be configured with a "double-write" or "sequential-write" pattern:

1. **Write 1:** Save the results to **S3/ADLS** in **Iceberg format**. This builds your long-term PRD foundation.
2. **Write 2:** Use the `spark.write.format("snowflake")` connector to push those same dataframes into **Snowflake STG**.

## Implementation Strategy

### Stage 1: The "Push" Model (Current)

During the validation phase, Databricks remains the "Writer."

* **Pros:** Easy to set up; Snowflake users get a standard table.
* **Cons:** You pay for egress/transfer and Snowflake storage.

### Stage 2: The "Open" Model (Target PRD)

Once stakeholders approve the data in Snowflake STG, you stop the Snowflake Spark write. Instead, you keep the Iceberg write in Databricks and:

1. **Create an External Volume** in Snowflake pointing to your Iceberg storage.
2. **Create Iceberg Tables** in Snowflake using the `BASE_LOCATION` and `METADATA_FILE_PATH`.

> **Peer Tip:** Ensure your Databricks cluster is using a consistent version of the Iceberg library that matches Snowflake’s supported Iceberg features (currently supporting Apache Iceberg v1.2+ in most regions).

## Comparison of Storage Approaches

| Feature | Phase 1 (Snowflake STG) | Phase 2 (Iceberg PRD) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Data Ownership** | Managed by Snowflake | Managed by You (S3/ADLS) |
| **Storage Cost** | Snowflake Storage Rates | Cloud Provider (S3/Blob) Rates |
| **Performance** | High (Native) | High (Metadata-based) |
| **Maintenance** | Auto-managed by Snowflake | Managed via Databricks maintenance (Vacuum/Optimize) |
